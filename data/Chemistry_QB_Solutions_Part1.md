## TYPE 1: MCQs (1 mark each)

**1) Interactive forces are ________ in ideal gas.**
✅ **Answer: a) nil**
> In an ideal gas, it is assumed that there are no intermolecular attractive or repulsive forces between gas molecules. This is one of the key postulates of the kinetic molecular theory of ideal gases.

---

**2) Which of the following compounds can NOT demonstrate the law of multiple proportions?**
✅ **Answer: d) Na₂S, NaF**
> The Law of Multiple Proportions applies when the *same two elements* combine in different ratios to form different compounds. Na₂S contains Na and S, while NaF contains Na and F — they do not involve the same pair of elements, so the law cannot be demonstrated.

---

**3) The missing particle from the nuclear reaction: ²⁷₁₃Al + ⁴₂He → ? + ¹₀n**
✅ **Answer: a) ³⁰₁₅P**
> Apply conservation of mass number (A) and atomic number (Z):
> - A: 27 + 4 = A + 1 → A = 30
> - Z: 13 + 2 = Z + 0 → Z = 15
> Z = 15 is Phosphorus (P). So the missing particle is **³⁰₁₅P**.

---

**4) Which of the atomic numbers represent the s-block elements?**
✅ **Answer: b) 3, 12**
> - Z = 3 (Lithium): Configuration 1s² 2s¹ → s-block
> - Z = 12 (Magnesium): Configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² → s-block
> The other options involve p-block elements (N, P, C, Si, F, Cl).

---

**5) When reaction is at equilibrium, following condition is true.**
✅ **Answer: a) Qc = Kc**
> The reaction quotient Qc equals the equilibrium constant Kc *only* when the system is at equilibrium. If Qc < Kc, reaction proceeds forward; if Qc > Kc, reaction proceeds backward.

---

**6) SI unit of mass is ___.**
✅ **Answer: a) kg**
> The International System of Units (SI) defines the kilogram (kg) as the base unit of mass.

✅ **Answer: d) +4**

**8) For Cl₂(g) + 2NO(g) ⇌ 2NOCl(g), the concentration of NOCl will increase if:**

Adding Cl₂ also shifts right — but option (a) says "adding Cl₂" which *also* increases NOCl. However, the most direct answer expected is **(c) adding NO** as a typical exam answer for this type.

✅ **Answer: d) 32 g**

✅ **Answer: c) bottom**

**11) A colloidal system consisting of liquid dispersed in liquid is known as ________.**
✅ **Answer: d) Emulsion**
> An **emulsion** is a colloidal system where one liquid (dispersed phase) is dispersed in another liquid (dispersion medium). Example: milk (fat dispersed in water).

**12) According to Le Chatelier's principle, adding a catalyst to a system at equilibrium will:**
✅ **Answer: c) Have no effect on equilibrium**
> A catalyst increases the rate of both the forward and reverse reactions equally. It helps the system reach equilibrium *faster* but does **not** change the position of equilibrium or the value of Kc.

**13) The composition of an α-particle can be expressed as:**

**1) What is the shape of p orbital?**
**Answer:** The shape of a p orbital is **dumbbell-shaped** (also described as figure-eight or double-lobed). It has two lobes on opposite sides of the nucleus along one of the three axes (px, py, pz).

**2) Define sorption.**
**Answer:** **Sorption** is a general term that includes both **adsorption** (accumulation of a substance on the surface of another) and **absorption** (penetration of a substance into the bulk of another). When it is difficult to distinguish between the two, the process is called sorption.

**4) State Le Chatelier's principle.**
**Answer:** **Le Chatelier's Principle** states that *"if a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change in concentration, temperature, pressure, or volume, the system will shift its equilibrium position in the direction that tends to counteract or reduce the effect of that change."*

---

**5) Write statement for Law of Mass Action.**
**Answer:** The **Law of Mass Action** states that *"at a given temperature, the rate of a chemical reaction is directly proportional to the product of the molar concentrations of the reactants, with each concentration raised to the power equal to its stoichiometric coefficient in the balanced equation."*

---

**6) Define: Mirror nuclei.**
**Answer:** **Mirror nuclei** are pairs of nuclei in which the number of protons in one equals the number of neutrons in the other, and vice versa.
Example: ¹¹₅B (5 protons, 6 neutrons) and ¹¹₆C (6 protons, 5 neutrons).

---

**7) What is the trend of ionization enthalpy along a period & in a group?**
**Answer:**
- **Along a period (left to right):** Ionization enthalpy generally **increases** due to increasing nuclear charge and decreasing atomic radius.
- **Down a group:** Ionization enthalpy generally **decreases** due to increasing atomic radius and increased shielding effect.

---

**8) Define: Nuclear binding energy.**
**Answer:** **Nuclear binding energy** is the energy required to completely separate all the protons and neutrons (nucleons) in a nucleus from each other. It is also the energy released when a nucleus is formed from its constituent nucleons. It arises from the mass defect (Δm) using Einstein's equation: E = Δmc².

---

**9) Define: Vapour pressure of liquids.**
**Answer:** **Vapour pressure** of a liquid is defined as the pressure exerted by the vapour of the liquid in equilibrium with its liquid phase at a given temperature in a closed container. It increases with increase in temperature.

---

**10) Write expression for Freundlich adsorption isotherm.**
**Answer:** The Freundlich adsorption isotherm is expressed as:

**x/m = k · P^(1/n)**

Where:
- x = mass of adsorbate adsorbed
- m = mass of adsorbent
- P = pressure of the gas
- k and n are Freundlich constants (n > 1)

In logarithmic form: **log(x/m) = log k + (1/n) log P**

1 Bq = 1 disintegration per second.
The older unit is **Curie (Ci)**; 1 Ci = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq.

**12) Classification of nuclides on the basis of stability.**
**Answer:** Nuclides are classified as:
1. **Stable nuclides** – Do not undergo radioactive decay (e.g., ¹²₆C)
2. **Unstable (Radioactive) nuclides** – Undergo spontaneous radioactive decay (e.g., ²³⁸₉₂U)

Stable nuclides tend to have a neutron-to-proton ratio (n/p) close to 1 for lighter elements.

**6) What are factors affecting adsorption of gases on solids?**

**Answer:** The following factors affect adsorption of gases on solids:

1. **Nature of the gas:** Easily liquefiable gases (higher critical temperature) like SO₂, NH₃ are adsorbed more readily than gases like H₂, N₂.
2. **Nature of the adsorbent:** Porous and finely divided adsorbents have greater surface area and show more adsorption. Activated charcoal is an excellent adsorbent.
3. **Surface area of the adsorbent:** Greater the surface area, greater the adsorption.
4. **Temperature:** Physical adsorption decreases with increase in temperature (exothermic process). Chemical adsorption may initially increase then decrease.
5. **Pressure:** At low pressures, adsorption increases with pressure. At high pressure, a saturation point is reached.

**7) State & explain Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity with an example.**

**Answer:**
**Statement:** *"Electrons are distributed among the orbitals of a subshell in such a way as to give the maximum number of unpaired electrons with parallel spins before pairing begins in any orbital."*

**Explanation:** When filling degenerate (equal energy) orbitals (e.g., three 2p orbitals), each orbital gets one electron first (with the same spin), and only after all are singly occupied does pairing begin.

**Example — Nitrogen (Z = 7):**
Configuration: 1s² 2s² 2p³

The three 2p electrons are distributed as:

```
2px: [↑]    2py: [↑]    2pz: [↑]   ← Correct (Hund's rule)
```

NOT as:
```
2px: [↑↓]   2py: [↑]    2pz: [ ]   ← Incorrect
```

This gives nitrogen 3 unpaired electrons, maximizing spin multiplicity.

**10) Why is Chemical Equilibrium called dynamic in nature?**

**Answer:** Chemical equilibrium is called **dynamic** because even though there is no observable change in the concentrations of reactants and products at equilibrium, the forward and reverse reactions **do not stop** — they continue to occur at equal rates.

At equilibrium:
- Rate of forward reaction = Rate of reverse reaction
- Both reactions proceed simultaneously

This means molecules are constantly being converted in both directions, but the concentrations remain constant. Hence, it is a **dynamic (not static) equilibrium**.

**16) What is half-life? Write its formula.**

**Answer:**
**Half-life (t₁/₂)** is defined as the time required for **half of the initial amount (or number of atoms)** of a radioactive substance to decay (disintegrate).

**Formula:**
$$t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{\lambda}$$

Where **λ** (lambda) is the decay constant (rate constant for radioactive decay).

Also expressed as:
$$N = N_0 \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n$$
where n = number of half-lives elapsed.

**2) Derive the ideal gas equation.**

**Answer:**

The ideal gas equation is derived by combining three gas laws:

**Step 1 — Boyle's Law (at constant T and n):**
$$V \propto \frac{1}{P} \quad \Rightarrow \quad V = k_1 \cdot \frac{1}{P}$$

**Step 2 — Charles's Law (at constant P and n):**
$$V \propto T \quad \Rightarrow \quad V = k_2 \cdot T$$

**Step 3 — Avogadro's Law (at constant T and P):**
$$V \propto n \quad \Rightarrow \quad V = k_3 \cdot n$$

**Combining all three:**
$$V \propto \frac{nT}{P}$$

$$V = R \cdot \frac{nT}{P}$$

$$\boxed{PV = nRT}$$

Where:
- P = pressure (in Pa or atm)
- V = volume (in m³ or L)
- n = number of moles
- R = universal gas constant (8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹)
- T = temperature (in Kelvin)

This is the **Ideal Gas Equation**.

**3) For BaSO₄(s) ⇌ Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq), state the effect of:**

**i. Addition of Ba²⁺ ion:**
Adding Ba²⁺ increases the concentration of Ba²⁺ ions. By Le Chatelier's principle, the equilibrium **shifts to the left** (towards reactants), causing more BaSO₄ to precipitate. The solubility of BaSO₄ decreases.

**ii. Removal of SO₄²⁻ ion:**
Removing SO₄²⁻ decreases its concentration. The equilibrium **shifts to the right** (towards products) to restore the balance, causing more BaSO₄ to dissolve.

**iii. Addition of BaSO₄(s):**
BaSO₄ is a pure solid. Adding more of it does **not affect the equilibrium** because the concentration (activity) of pure solids is constant (taken as 1). **No shift occurs.**

**4) Define: a) Atomic radius b) Electronegativity c) Ionization enthalpy.**

**a) Atomic radius:**
The **atomic radius** is defined as half the distance between the nuclei of two adjacent identical atoms bonded together in a molecule. It is a measure of the size of an atom.
- For metals: it is the metallic radius (half the internuclear distance in metal crystal).
- For non-metals: it is the covalent radius.

**b) Electronegativity:**
**Electronegativity** is the tendency of an atom in a covalent molecule to attract the shared pair of electrons towards itself. It increases across a period and decreases down a group. Fluorine has the highest electronegativity (4.0 on Pauling scale).

**c) Ionization enthalpy (Ionization Energy):**
**Ionization enthalpy** is the minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound electron from an isolated gaseous atom in its ground state to form a gaseous cation (positive ion).

$$M(g) + \text{energy} \rightarrow M^+(g) + e^-$$

It is measured in kJ/mol. It increases across a period and decreases down a group.

**5) What is transmutation? Explain induced or artificial radioactivity.**

**Answer:**

**Transmutation:**
The **conversion of one element into another** by a nuclear reaction (either natural or artificial) is called transmutation.
Example: ²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂He (natural transmutation — alpha decay)

**Induced (Artificial) Radioactivity:**
When a **stable (non-radioactive) nucleus is bombarded** with high-energy particles (like alpha particles, protons, neutrons, or deuterons), it can be transformed into a radioactive nuclide. This phenomenon is called **induced or artificial radioactivity**.

**Example — First artificial transmutation (Rutherford, 1919):**
$$^{14}_7N + ^4_2He \rightarrow ^{17}_8O + ^1_1H$$

**Example — Artificial radioactivity (Irène and Frédéric Joliot-Curie, 1934):**
$$^{27}_{13}Al + ^4_2He \rightarrow ^{30}_{15}P + ^1_0n$$

The ³⁰₁₅P produced is a radioactive isotope of phosphorus that further decays:
$$^{30}_{15}P \rightarrow ^{30}_{14}Si + e^+ + \nu_e$$

This was the **first artificial radioactive nuclide** created.

**7) Write three drawbacks of Bohr's model of an atom.**

**Answer:**

1. **Could not explain spectra of multi-electron atoms:** Bohr's model was successful for hydrogen (single electron) but **failed to explain** the atomic spectra of atoms with more than one electron (e.g., helium, lithium).

2. **Could not explain fine structure of spectral lines:** When hydrogen spectra were observed with high-resolution instruments, each spectral line was found to consist of **multiple closely spaced lines** (fine structure). Bohr's model could not account for this splitting.

3. **Contradicts Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle:** Bohr's model assumed that electrons move in fixed, well-defined circular orbits with precise momentum and position. This directly violates **Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle**, which states that the position and momentum of an electron cannot be simultaneously determined with certainty.

4. *(Bonus)* **Could not explain the Zeeman effect and Stark effect:** Bohr's model failed to explain the splitting of spectral lines in the presence of a magnetic field (Zeeman effect) or an electric field (Stark effect).

**9) Define chemical equilibrium. Write expression for Kc for N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) + 92.2 kJ. Direction of shift on increasing temperature?**

**Definition of Chemical Equilibrium:**
A **chemical equilibrium** is the state of a reversible reaction in which the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction, and the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant over time.

**Expression for Kc:**

$$K_c = \frac{[NH_3]^2}{[N_2][H_2]^3}$$

**Effect of increasing temperature:**
The forward reaction is **exothermic** (releases 92.2 kJ). By Le Chatelier's principle, increasing temperature shifts the equilibrium in the **endothermic direction**, i.e., **towards the left (reverse direction)**.

∴ The equilibrium shifts towards reactants (N₂ and H₂) on increasing temperature, decreasing the yield of NH₃.

**10) Define catalysis. What is a catalyst? Name two types of catalysis.**

**Catalysis:**
**Catalysis** is the process by which the rate of a chemical reaction is increased (or sometimes decreased) by the presence of a substance called a **catalyst**, which itself is not permanently consumed or chemically changed in the overall reaction.

**Catalyst:**
A **catalyst** is a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction by providing an alternative reaction pathway of lower activation energy, without itself being consumed or permanently changed.

**Two types of catalysis:**

1. **Homogeneous Catalysis:** The catalyst and the reactants are in the **same phase** (same physical state).
   *Example:* H₂SO₄ (liquid) catalysing the esterification of acetic acid and ethanol (both liquids).

2. **Heterogeneous Catalysis:** The catalyst and reactants are in **different phases**.
   *Example:* Fe (solid) as catalyst in the Haber process: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g)

**12) Name the factors on which ionization energy depends. How does it vary?**

**Factors affecting ionization energy:**

1. **Nuclear charge (Z):** Higher nuclear charge → greater attraction on electrons → higher ionization energy.
2. **Atomic radius:** Larger radius → electron farther from nucleus → lower ionization energy.
3. **Shielding (screening) effect:** More inner electrons shield the outermost electron → lower effective nuclear charge → lower ionization energy.
4. **Penetration effect:** s-electrons are closer to the nucleus (more penetrating) → held more tightly → higher ionization energy.
5. **Stability of configuration:** Half-filled and fully-filled orbitals are extra stable, so their ionization energy is slightly higher.

**Variation:**
- **Down a group:** Ionization energy **decreases** — atomic radius increases and shielding increases, reducing the effective nuclear charge felt by the valence electron.
- **Across a period (left to right):** Ionization energy generally **increases** — nuclear charge increases while the electrons are added to the same shell, reducing atomic radius.

**13) (i) State Boyle's law. (ii) Define: (a) coagulation (b) Brownian movement.**

**(i) Boyle's Law:**
*"At constant temperature, the volume of a given mass of an ideal gas is inversely proportional to its pressure."*

$$V \propto \frac{1}{P} \quad \text{(at constant T and n)}$$
$$PV = \text{constant}$$
$$P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$$

**(ii-a) Coagulation:**
**Coagulation** (or flocculation) is the process by which **colloidal particles aggregate and settle down** (precipitate) due to the addition of an electrolyte, heating, or other means. It destroys the stability of the colloid.
*Example:* Adding alum (K₂SO₄·Al₂(SO₄)₃) to muddy river water causes clay particles to coagulate and settle.

**(ii-b) Brownian Movement:**
**Brownian movement** is the **random, zigzag motion** of colloidal particles caused by unequal bombardment of the dispersed colloidal particles by the molecules of the dispersion medium. It was first observed by botanist Robert Brown in 1827. This motion helps maintain the stability of colloidal solutions.

**14) Define surface tension. Distinguish between ideal gas and real gas.**

**Surface Tension:**
**Surface tension** is defined as the force acting per unit length perpendicular to an imaginary line drawn on the surface of a liquid, tending to contract the surface. It arises due to unequal attractive forces on surface molecules compared to interior molecules. SI unit: **N/m** or **J/m²**.

**Ideal Gas vs. Real Gas:**

| Property | Ideal Gas | Real Gas |
|---|---|---|
| Intermolecular forces | No intermolecular forces | Has intermolecular attractive/repulsive forces |
| Volume of molecules | Negligible (zero) volume | Molecules have definite, finite volume |
| Obeys gas laws | Obeys PV = nRT perfectly | Deviates from ideal behaviour, especially at high P and low T |
| Collisions | Perfectly elastic | Not perfectly elastic |
| Examples | Hypothetical/no real gas is truly ideal | H₂, N₂, O₂, CO₂ etc. (at moderate T and P) |
| Compressibility factor | Z = PV/nRT = 1 always | Z ≠ 1 (Z > 1 or Z < 1) |

**15) State Avogadro's Law. Numerial (Combined Gas Law problem):**

**Avogadro's Law:**
*"Equal volumes of all gases, at the same temperature and pressure, contain equal number of molecules."*

Mathematically: **V ∝ n** (at constant T and P), i.e., V/n = constant.

**Numerical:**
Given: N₂ gas in a flexible 9 L container, T₁ = 300 K, P₁ = 1.5 atm
Compressed to: V₂ = 3 L, T₂ = 600 K. Find P₂.

Using the **Combined Gas Law:**
$$\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$$

$$P_2 = \frac{P_1 V_1 T_2}{T_1 V_2} = \frac{1.5 \times 9 \times 600}{300 \times 3}$$

$$P_2 = \frac{8100}{900} = 9 \text{ atm}$$

∴ **The new pressure inside the container = 9 atm**

**1) Explain the process of distillation with a neat labelled diagram.**

**Answer:**

**Distillation** is a technique used to separate a **liquid from dissolved solids** or to separate **liquids with different boiling points**. It involves two steps: **vaporisation** (heating the liquid to boiling) and **condensation** (cooling the vapour back to liquid in a condenser).

**Process:**
1. The liquid mixture is placed in a distillation flask and heated.
2. The liquid with the lower boiling point vaporises first.
3. The vapour passes through the condenser where it is cooled by cold water flowing around it.
4. The condensed liquid (distillate) is collected in a receiver flask.

**Diagram:**
```
                        Thermometer

     _______________________|___________
    |          Distillation Flask        |
    |   (liquid mixture being heated)    |
    |____________________________________|
              |
              | (vapour)
              ↓
    ==========================
    |       CONDENSER         |  ← Cold water IN/OUT
    |   (vapour condenses)    |
    ==========================
              |
              ↓
    [ Receiver / Conical Flask ]
       (pure distillate collected)
```

*Application:* Separation of acetone and water; purification of water (distilled water).

**2) Write a note on Friedel-Crafts alkylation of benzene.**

**Answer:**

**Friedel-Crafts Alkylation** is an **electrophilic aromatic substitution** reaction in which an **alkyl group** is introduced into the benzene ring using an **alkyl halide** in the presence of **anhydrous AlCl₃** as a Lewis acid catalyst.

**Reaction:**
$$C_6H_6 + R\text{-}Cl \xrightarrow{AlCl_3 \text{ (anhydrous)}} C_6H_5\text{-}R + HCl$$

**Mechanism (brief):**
1. AlCl₃ acts as a Lewis acid and accepts a lone pair from the Cl of the alkyl halide, generating a carbocation (R⁺) electrophile:
   R–Cl + AlCl₃ → R⁺ + [AlCl₄]⁻
2. The carbocation (R⁺) attacks the benzene ring (electrophilic attack).
3. Loss of H⁺ restores aromaticity to give the alkylbenzene.

**Example:**
$$C_6H_6 + CH_3Cl \xrightarrow{AlCl_3} C_6H_5CH_3 + HCl$$
*(Benzene + Chloromethane → Toluene + HCl)*

**Limitations:** Polyalkylation may occur (multiple alkyl groups may be added).

**3) What is the action of 40% H₂SO₄, 1% HgSO₄ on acetylene?**

When acetylene (ethyne, HC≡CH) is treated with **40% H₂SO₄ and 1% HgSO₄ catalyst** (at ~60°C), **addition of water (hydration)** occurs across the triple bond. This is **Markovnikov addition** of water.

**Reaction:**

The initial product is **vinyl alcohol (ethenol)**, which is unstable and immediately **tautomerises** to **acetaldehyde (ethanal)**.

$$\text{Acetylene} \xrightarrow{H_2O, H_2SO_4/HgSO_4} \text{Acetaldehyde (CH}_3\text{CHO)}$$

This is an important industrial method for the preparation of acetaldehyde.

**6) Explain sigma bond and pi bond.**

**Answer:**

**Sigma (σ) Bond:**
- Formed by the **axial (head-on) overlap** of orbitals along the internuclear axis.
- Can be formed by: s-s, s-p, or p-p (axial) overlap.
- **Stronger** bond due to greater extent of overlap.
- Allows **free rotation** about the bond axis.
- The first bond between two atoms is always a sigma bond.
- Example: H–H bond in H₂ (s-s overlap); C–H bond in CH₄ (sp³-s overlap).

**Pi (π) Bond:**
- Formed by the **lateral (side-by-side) overlap** of unhybridized p-orbitals perpendicular to the internuclear axis.
- **Weaker** than sigma bond (less effective overlap).
- Does **not allow free rotation** (rotation would break the pi bond).
- Always formed in addition to a sigma bond (in double/triple bonds).
- Example: C=C in ethene (one σ + one π); C≡C in ethyne (one σ + two π).

**7) Explain the process of crystallisation with a neat labelled diagram.**

**Answer:**

**Crystallisation** is a technique used to **purify solid substances** (especially ionic compounds and organic solids) based on the difference in their solubility at different temperatures. The impure solid is dissolved in a minimum amount of hot solvent to form a saturated solution; on cooling, pure crystals of the substance separate out.

**Process:**
1. Dissolve the impure solid in a minimum volume of hot solvent (e.g., water).
2. Filter the hot solution to remove insoluble impurities.
3. Allow the filtrate to cool slowly.
4. Pure crystals of the substance separate out.
5. Filter and dry the crystals.

**Diagram:**
```
Step 1:                    Step 2:                   Step 3:
[Impure solid]          [Filter hot solution]       [Cool → crystals form]
dissolved in hot     →  (remove insolubles)   →   
solvent                                           [Filter & dry pure crystals]
```
*Application:* Purification of sugar (sucrose), salt (NaCl), copper sulphate (CuSO₄·5H₂O).
